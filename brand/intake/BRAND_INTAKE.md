## 1. Purpose & positioning (from questionnaire)
- One sentence: *"LifeLink is a Muslim marriage app that connects compatible individuals through trusted, meaningful introductions without endless swiping or in-app messaging."*
- Core brand statement: **"Built for introductions. Designed for marriage."**
- Supporting messages: Compatibility before conversation · Meaningful introductions. Genuine intentions · Less swiping. More purpose · Your journey to marriage, made more meaningful · Connecting people, not collecting matches.
- Fundamental belief: *"Finding a spouse should be about finding the right person, not collecting the most matches."*
- Four principles: (1) Introductions, not conversations (2) Compatibility before attraction (3) Technology combined with human matchmaking (4) A premium, trustworthy experience.
- "We're not trying to replace tradition. We're trying to modernise the process while preserving what matters."

## 2. Audience
- Primary: single Muslims genuinely seeking marriage, ~21–40, UK first, then international. Includes young and established professionals, graduates, reverts, divorcees, single parents, people without large traditional networks.
- Secondary: families and guardians.
- Diverse cultures, backgrounds and levels of practice. Welcoming, never judgemental or exclusive.
- Platforms: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Snapchat (so portrait adaptations matter).

## 3. Personality
- Three words: **Trustworthy. Intentional. Refined.**
- Warm, intelligent, approachable, quietly confident; a trusted friend with a matchmaker's professionalism and discretion.
- Never: overly formal, preachy, judgemental, casual, flirtatious, playful or gimmicky.
- "Premium but never intimidating, modern but never disconnected from faith, sophisticated without feeling cold."
- Admired brands: Apple (simplicity, polish), Airbnb (trust between strangers), Aesop (understated luxury, restraint), Monzo (making a serious process approachable).

## 4. Explicit visual and verbal prohibitions
- Excessive hearts, wedding rings, predictable romantic imagery
- Generic Islamic patterns; overused crescent-moon branding
- Cheesy romantic language, unrealistic promises
- Dating-app language ("swipe right", "find your perfect match")
- Playful, gimmicky or gamified treatments
- Compatibility percentages or guarantees
- Outdated or overly traditional looks that feel disconnected from younger generations

## 6. Editorial references (from artboard image)
1. **"ORRAV" folder**: royal-blue textured card, blind-embossed ornament, cream index tab. *Reads as: tactile, crafted, stationery-grade, quiet luxury.*
2. **"Save the date"**: oversized red display type with blackletter "The" on oxblood/cream, small photo. *Reads as: confident editorial scale contrast, mixed typefaces.*
3. **"The Wedi Agency"**: condensed high-contrast serif with script overlay on a muted sepia photo. *Reads as: cinematic, script crossing a structural typeface.*
Common thread: wedding-stationery craft, paper, emboss, ink, bold scale contrast. Note: the references include couples and wedding imagery. Imagery rules are not yet stated (see open questions).
